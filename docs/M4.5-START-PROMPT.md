## Important accepted behavior

- The player remains at the shot origin while the client-only camera follows the ball.
- After camera restoration, the player walks or flies to the resting ball.
- Do not add automatic teleport-to-ball in M4.5.
- The travel concern is recorded for optional on-demand evaluation during M6.
- Practice shots remain available when no hole session is active.
- Gameplay state and scoring remain server-owned.
- Client messages/HUD display authoritative state; clients must not calculate outcomes.

## Why M4.5 exists

M4 mechanics work, but manual testing showed the one-player experience still depends too heavily on developer intervention:

- Test terrain had to be prepared manually.
- Fixed default coordinates may land in ocean, underground, or unsafe terrain.
- Clubs had to be manually granted.
- Commands/chat carry too much of the user experience.
- Practice balls and active-hole balls may be confusing.
- Lifecycle recovery needs hardening before multiplayer multiplies the complexity.

## M4.5 target

A player can launch the mod, start and finish a configured hole repeatedly without developer assistance, understand the complete hole state through the UI, and recover safely from expected failures.

## Recommended M4.5 scope

### 1. Self-contained single-player flow

- Start, restart, abandon, status, and Pick Up behavior.
- Ensure required equipment is available or provide clear in-game guidance.
- Avoid operator-only setup for ordinary play.
- Make repeated hole attempts clean and predictable.

### 2. Practice versus hole play

- Make the current mode obvious.
- Prevent the practice-ball hotkey from creating confusing competing balls during an active hole.
- Preserve unrestricted practice when no hole is active.
- Keep server validation authoritative.

### 3. Player-facing hole HUD

- Hole number and par.
- Current strokes and Double Par + 2 cap.
- Penalties and score-to-par.
- Active/completed state and final golf result.
- Do not rely solely on transient chat.
- Use typed server-to-client state synchronization if needed; clients display rather than own state.

### 4. Lifecycle and failure hardening

- Restart/abandon cleans up assigned balls and session state.
- Missing or removed active ball fails safely and offers recovery.
- Dimension/configuration errors are actionable.
- Define safe single-player disconnect/reconnect behavior.
- No stale ball or score may leak into a new attempt.

### 5. Repeatable development/test hole

- Provide a reliable dry test experience without manually constructing a grass platform every session.
- Do not create a general course-authoring system.
- Do not destructively rewrite arbitrary player terrain without explicit design justification.
- Keep daytime/weather controls development-only.
- Document configuration and reset behavior.

### 6. Gameplay refinement

- Preserve accepted cup speed and Putter tuning.
- Verify short and long putts, penalties, cap, Pick Up, restart, and camera restoration.
- Record additional subjective findings without expanding scope silently.

## Known environment notes

- Loom clients use development identities and cannot join the normal online-mode Docker server.
- For local manual testing, use a temporary offline-only Loom server without weakening the tracked Docker server’s authentication.
- Restore temporary `run/server.properties` security changes after testing.
- The optional Linux `flite` narrator library warning and development auth 401 warnings are environmental, not mod failures.
- The background console harness may report “Unknown or incomplete command” after successful commands because it sends an extra blank line; inspect the preceding command result before treating it as a failure.
