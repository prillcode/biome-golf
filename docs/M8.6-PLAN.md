# M8.6 - Concurrent Rounds, Join Clarity, and Scoring UX

**Status:** Complete. See `docs/M8.6-CLOSEOUT.md`. The implementation, automated/Loom/
Docker verification, installed-client deployment, and family playtest acceptance passed.
Baseline: the post-M8 authored-course and M8.5 lobby/menu work committed through
`f65ca05`.

M8.6 removes the server-wide single-round restriction exposed by family playtesting. It
also fixes the misleading create/join guidance around a round that has already started,
replaces active-hole score-to-par with an actual shots-attempted display, and closes the
completed-solo reconnect crash found in the same server log review.

This is a bounded lifecycle and presentation milestone. It does not add matchmaking,
late joining, alternate game modes, or persistent rounds.

M8.6 follows the architectural preference in
`docs/SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE-CLIENT-LIGHT.md`: implement the complete round-management
path through server-owned state and vanilla-compatible commands/messages first, then
layer the existing custom Java screens and HUD on top as optional enhanced presentation.
M8.6 does not claim to make an unmodified client compatible with the whole golf game;
the existing custom ball entity, items/blocks, shot input, camera, and other client
dependencies require the separate assessment requested by that document.

## Purpose

Allow independent golfers and groups to play concurrently on a shared server, including
on the same course and the same hole, while preserving server authority, player/ball
isolation, Ready Golf barriers, and authored-course behavior.

The required player experience is:

1. More than one Ready Golf lobby or playing round may exist at the same time.
2. Solo play may coexist with Ready Golf rounds.
3. A player may belong to only one solo attempt, lobby, or round at a time.
4. Players may create rounds on different courses or on the same course.
5. Separate rounds may occupy the same hole; M8.6 adds no hole reservation or tee-time
   system.
6. A lobby remains joinable only until its coordinator starts it.
7. When more than one lobby is open, the player chooses a specific lobby rather than
   relying on one global active round.
8. A join attempt against a started round explains that late joining is disabled and
   directs the player to an open lobby or creation of another round.
9. During an active hole, vanilla-compatible status output and the optional HUD show
   accepted shots attempted on that hole instead of a premature score relative to par.
10. Reconnecting with a completed solo scorecard never fails because global course
    selection is absent or has changed.
11. A player can discover, create, join, start, inspect, and leave rounds through normal
    server commands and vanilla-compatible chat without using a custom screen.
12. The custom Java browser and HUD consume the same server-owned state as optional
    enhancements; they are not the only path through the round lifecycle.

## Playtest Evidence

The 2026-09-17 Docker log established the triggering sequence:

- `pomzii_YT` created a lobby at `01:16:59`, `PrLLager207` joined at `01:17:13`, and the
  two-player round started and played successfully.
- `PrLLager207` created a new lobby at `01:38:49` and started it with one golfer at
  `01:39:33`.
- `pomzii_YT` attempted to join at `01:39:36`, after the roster had closed. The server
  answered `no open Ready Golf lobby; use /golf round create`.
- Creating then failed because the same global `activeRound` already existed. The two
  individually correct rejections formed an impossible and contradictory recovery loop.
- At `01:05:14` and `01:12:16`, `PrLLager207` reconnect attempts failed with `Invalid

The active-hole HUD also displays `Score: -3` after the first accepted shot on a par-4
hole. That is arithmetic score-to-par, but it reads like a finished-hole result before
the hole is complete.

## Existing Seams and Required Change

- `ReadyGolfRound` already models one independent 1-4 player round and should remain the
  Minecraft-free authority for that round.
- `ActiveHoleService.activeRound` is the server-wide bottleneck. Round creation,
  membership lookup, course-state updates, transitions, reconnect handling, lobby
  snapshots, scorecards, and cleanup all dereference this one field.
- `HoleLifecycle` already keys the active hole session by player UUID. It can continue to
  own one assigned session per player, provided round resolution becomes player-scoped.
- `soloCourseStates` already supports more than one player, but global `activeRound`
  checks currently block solo play whenever any Ready Golf round exists and can suppress
  scoring for nonparticipants.
- `CourseListPayload`, `LobbyStatePayload`, and `RoundLobbyActionPayload` currently assume
  at most one compatible lobby. Their versioned replacements must carry a server-issued
  round identity where an action or display refers to a particular lobby.
- The existing course browser and lobby screens require custom Fabric payloads. They must
  become optional adapters over a server-owned lobby projection rather than the only way
  to discover or operate concurrent rounds.
- `CourseBlockBreakGuard` currently derives protection from one globally configured
  course/hole. Concurrent rounds on different courses require protection to stop
  depending on one selected course.
- `HoleStatePayload` already carries total strokes and penalty strokes, but the domain
  does not retain accepted shots independently. `strokes - penaltyStrokes` is incorrect
  after Pick Up replaces total strokes with the cap. M8.6 must add an explicit,
  authoritative accepted-shot count that Pick Up does not inflate; the client must not
  derive or invent a different scoring model.
- `sendSoloScorecard` must resolve pars from the completed player's own course state, not
  from `ActiveHoleService.course`.

Do not create a second Ready Golf state machine or place gameplay authority in a client
round browser. Introduce the smallest server-owned round registry around the existing
`ReadyGolfRound` domain object and route existing operations through it.

## Locked Gameplay Contracts

### Round Identity and Membership

- Every Ready Golf lobby/round has its existing UUID identity. Network and service
  actions target that identity; the UI may show a short display form but must send the
  complete server-issued identity.
- The server owns the round registry and a player-to-round membership index.
- A player may own exactly one golf context at a time: one solo course attempt or one
  Ready Golf membership. Practice mode owns neither.
- The player membership index contains only `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` participants.
  Historical `WITHDRAWN` participant rows may remain in an immutable round snapshot for
  ordering/results, but they do not own a context and cannot block that player from
  creating or joining another round.
- `ReadyGolfRound.MAX_PARTICIPANTS` remains four per round. M8.6 introduces no arbitrary
  server-wide round cap; ordinary server resource limits remain applicable.
- Multiple lobbies and playing rounds may use the same course and same hole.
- Starting one lobby closes only that lobby's roster. It must not close, start, alter, or
  hide another lobby.
- Late joining a playing or completed round remains disallowed. Catch-up, inherited
  scores, and mid-hole entry are out of scope.
- Completed rounds remain owned by their connected remaining participants until replay,
  explicit leave, or disconnect. Disconnecting after completion preserves current M6
  behavior and is treated as leaving the completed round rather than suspension. Completed
  rounds do not block unrelated players from creating another round.

### Lobby Discovery and Fallback Commands

- The server owns one display-safe lobby/course projection containing every open lobby's
  round identity, course, coordinator display name, participant count, and capacity, plus
  available finalized courses.
- `/golf round list` renders that projection through vanilla-compatible chat. Each lobby
  entry includes a clickable join action where supported and a visible command fallback.
- The custom Java browser renders the same projection through versioned optional payloads;
  it must not maintain a separate lobby-discovery policy.
- Creating a round always specifies or resolves one finalized course and returns the
  newly created round identity in authoritative state.
- `/golf round create <courseId>`, `/golf round join <roundId>`, `/golf round start`,
  `/golf round leave`, and `/golf round status` form a complete non-GUI lifecycle.
- Joining from the UI targets one listed round identity, not merely a course ID. Two open
  lobbies on the same course must remain distinguishable.
- `/golf round join <roundId>` is the unambiguous command path, not merely a diagnostic
  fallback.
- Bare `/golf round join` may remain as convenience only when exactly one open lobby
  exists. With zero lobbies it directs the player to create/browse; with multiple lobbies
  it directs the player to list/browse and choose one.
- Rejections distinguish `no open lobby`, `round already started`, `round complete`,
  `round full`, and `player already has an active golf context`. No message may direct a
  player to an action that the same state necessarily rejects.

### Server-First Presentation Boundary

- Core round participation must not require a custom Fabric screen or serverbound custom
  payload. Every browser/lobby action exposed by the enhanced Java client has an
  equivalent server command using the same service method and validation.
- Server-generated chat, action-bar, title, boss-bar, or scoreboard output is the
  compatibility presentation. Custom Java screens/HUD may replace or augment it but may
  not become the sole source of required instructions or state.
- Build lobby/course display data once on the server and adapt it to chat and optional
  payloads. Do not put filtering, joinability, capacity, coordinator, or phase decisions
  in client code.
- Custom serverbound payloads remain bounded convenience intent for enhanced clients.
  They must invoke the same authoritative operations as commands and cannot submit
  participant lists, phase changes, scores, or outcomes.
- Absence of payload capability must not prevent command-driven round creation, joining,
  starting, status inspection, advancement, or leaving.
- M8.6 verifies this server-first interaction seam, but full unmodified Java/Geyser play
  remains blocked by pre-existing custom-content and shot-input dependencies outside this
  milestone. Record those blockers; do not mask them or broaden M8.6 into the full
  client-light migration.

### Concurrent Play and Isolation

- Server authority, Ready Golf no-turn behavior, per-round all-active-terminal barriers,
  and player-owned balls remain unchanged.
- Every shot, penalty, cup event, restart, Pick Up, transition, result, scorecard,
  disconnect, reconnect, withdrawal, and cleanup resolves the caller/owner's round before
  mutation.
- A terminal event in one round cannot open the barrier, transition the hole, complete
  the course, send results, or clean balls in another round.
- Solo progression remains player-scoped and works while any number of unrelated Ready
  Golf rounds exist.
- Balls from different rounds remain visible through normal entity synchronization.
  Existing ownership rules prevent a player from striking another player's ball.
- Same-hole concurrency is allowed despite possible visual overlap. Ball collision,
  colored identifiers, tee-time scheduling, and hole occupancy reservations are not
  introduced in this milestone.
- Cup and flag placement is shared world state and must remain idempotent when multiple
  rounds prepare the same authored hole.

### Disconnect, Reconnect, and Runtime Lifetime

- Disconnect and reconnect rules remain those of M6, scoped to the player's actual
  round: suspend before that round advances, reconnect to the same state when valid, and
  withdraw stale suspended state when that round advances without the player.
- A disconnect from a `COMPLETE` round is a permanent leave from that completed round.
  It removes the player's membership index entry and does not create reconnectable
  suspended state. Remaining connected participants may still replay or leave.
- Disconnecting from one lobby or round cannot change another round's coordinator,
  barrier, participant count, or lifecycle.
- Empty/fully withdrawn rounds are removed independently. Cleanup must never sweep
  sessions or balls belonging to another round.
- Active rounds remain runtime-only and are cancelled on server restart. M8.6 does not
  add round persistence.
- Join/reconnect callbacks must not throw because a global course selection is null or
  different from the course retained in player state.

### Course Selection, Authoring, and Protection

- Round-scoped course selection remains independent of the operator's global/default
  course selection.
- Existing solo/default selection behavior remains compatible, but no Ready Golf action
  may mutate global selection as a side effect.
- Authoring mutations may conservatively remain blocked while any active golf play
  exists. Per-course live editing is not required by M8.6.
- `CourseBlockBreakGuard` owns a dimension-keyed, immutable protected-zone index covering
  every finalized authored course plus any supported configured development hole, rather
  than whichever course happens to be globally selected. Initialize or replace the index
  when authored-course state loads and refresh it after finalize/delete and supported
  development-hole reconfiguration. Overlapping zones are harmless; never scan world
  regions on block break.
- Audit authoring, deletion, global/default selection, `initialize`, `initializeCourse`,
  development-hole commands, and server-stop reset entry points. Conservatively reject
  authoring/deletion/reinitialization while any active golf context exists unless the
  operation has explicitly scoped cancellation and cleanup semantics.
- Operator/dev exemptions and repair workflows remain unchanged.

### Active-Hole HUD Semantics

- During `ACTIVE` and `MISSING_BALL`, neither the compatibility presentation nor the
  enhanced HUD renders the current hole's relative-to-par result (`Score: -3`, for
  example).
- Replace that active-hole score row with `Shots attempted: N`, where `N` is the number
  of accepted shot executions and excludes water/out-of-bounds penalty strokes.
- Keep total authoritative `Strokes: total / limit`, because the stroke limit includes
  penalties and remains relevant to completion.
- Keep penalty count when nonzero.
- Relative-to-par and golf terminology remain visible when the hole is terminal and on
  final/per-hole scorecards.
- While the current hole is active, cumulative course relative-to-par includes completed
  holes only. Omit that row before the first hole is complete; never subtract the active
  hole's par from in-progress strokes. When the current hole becomes terminal, include it
  in the completed-hole course total.
- The server publishes the same display-ready shots-attempted, total-strokes, penalty,
  and completion state through a vanilla-compatible channel, initially action bar and
  explicit `/golf hole status` output. The enhanced Java HUD consumes the same values.
- The client may format server-supplied state but must not become scoring authority.

   owner travel, HUD state, strokes, penalties, and barriers.
7. Complete a hole in one round while the other remains active; verify only the completed
   round receives results/advance availability.
8. Advance one round and verify the other remains on its own hole.
9. Leave or complete one round and create a new round without disturbing the other.
10. Exercise disconnect/reconnect in one round while the other continues.
11. Run one solo attempt while a Ready Golf round exists and verify both progress.
12. Verify action-bar and `/golf hole status` values after a first shot on par 4 and after
    a penalty: shots attempted excludes penalties, total strokes includes them, and no
    active `Score: -3` appears. Confirm the optional HUD agrees.
13. Reconnect a player with a completed solo course while no global course is selected;
    verify connection and final scorecard delivery succeed without server exceptions.
14. Repeat create/list/join/start/status/leave using the optional Java browser/HUD and
    confirm it produces the same server state and rejection behavior as commands.

## Risk Register and Stop Conditions

| Risk | Mitigation / proof |
|---|---|
| Registry and membership index drift apart | Encapsulate both in one pure domain type; mutate atomically; invariant tests after rejected and successful operations. |
| Global `activeRound` assumptions survive the refactor | Audit every reference and classify it as target-round, player-round, all-rounds, or solo-only; tests run two rounds through every lifecycle event. |
| A transition or cleanup touches another round's ball | Resolve round from participant/owner first and retain player UUID plus assigned ball UUID checks; concurrent transition tests and LAN observation. |
| Same-course rounds interfere through shared cup blocks | Keep cup placement idempotent and scoring keyed to assigned ball/session; plain-Java owner-routing tests plus same-hole Loom/Docker/manual observation. |
| Solo play is suppressed whenever any Ready Golf round exists | Replace global participation checks with player membership checks; concurrent solo + two-round tests. |
| UI joins the wrong same-course lobby | Carry full server-issued round UUID in browser entries and actions; do not identify a lobby by course ID. |
| Custom browser becomes mandatory for lobby discovery | Provide `/golf round list` plus complete create/join/start/status/leave commands first; derive chat and optional payloads from one server projection. |
| Custom HUD becomes the only usable scoring display | Publish the same server-owned values through action bar and `/golf hole status`; keep `HoleStatePayload` as optional enhanced presentation. |
| Stale clients decode changed payload layouts | Use version-changed payload identifiers and capability checks, following the M7 payload-evolution lesson. |
| Reconnect callback disconnects a player on presentation failure | Remove global course dependency, test completed-state snapshots, and keep join handling fail-safe without hiding state corruption. |
| Protecting all authored courses adds per-break overhead | `CourseBlockBreakGuard` owns a dimension-keyed immutable metadata-derived index refreshed on load/finalize/delete/dev-hole changes; never scan blocks or regions. |
| Active score display remains misleading around penalties or Pick Up | Store accepted attempts explicitly and test attempts, total strokes, penalties, Pick Up, and completed-hole-only cumulative scoring as separate values. |
| Scope expands into public matchmaking | Provide server-local lobby discovery only; no queue, invitations, permissions, backend, or persistence. |

Stop and consult the user before implementation proceeds if:

- concurrent rounds require weakening server authority or accepting client-authored round,
  score, ball, or membership state;
- safe isolation appears to require a second physics/entity path rather than owner/session
  routing;
- same-hole concurrency requires changing Minecraft collision behavior or introducing a
  reservation system to remain correct;
- the refactor requires persistent active rounds, external storage, matchmaking, or a
  permissions framework;
- Fabric APIs are insufficient and a new Mixin is proposed without architectural
  justification;
- the committed post-M8/M8.5 baseline conflicts with these contracts and cannot be
  reconciled without discarding user changes; or
- any verification rung fails and cannot be repaired within the current slice.

## Non-Goals

Do not implement:

- late joining or spectator-to-player promotion after a round starts;
- tee times, hole reservations, or exclusion of different rounds from the same hole;
- explicit ready toggles;
- matchmaking queues, invitations, private/password lobbies, or public-server backend
  infrastructure;
- active-round persistence across server restarts;
- cross-round tournaments, leaderboards, or global statistics;
- ball-to-ball collision, colored ball cosmetics, or expanded identification systems;
- Scramble, traditional turns, Match Play, Best Ball, or other M9 game modes;
- wind, advanced lies, cinematic camera, landing prediction, chip shots, expanded clubs,
  or multiple tee boxes; or
- a new course-authoring or world-protection framework.

M8.6 also does not perform the broader client-light migration of the golf ball entity,
club/cup/flag content, swing controls, camera, networking baseline, or resource packs.
Those remain inputs to the separate architectural assessment in
`docs/SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE-CLIENT-LIGHT.md`.

## Final Success Criteria

M8.6 is complete when:

1. At least two Ready Golf lobbies/rounds can coexist server-side.
2. Separate rounds can use different courses, the same course, and the same hole.
3. Solo play works while unrelated Ready Golf rounds are active.
4. Every player belongs to at most one golf context.
5. Joining targets a specific open lobby and started-round rejection gives actionable,
   non-contradictory guidance.
6. Players can list, create, join, start, inspect, and leave rounds through server
   commands and vanilla-compatible chat without a custom screen or serverbound payload.
7. The custom Java browser and HUD consume the same server-owned projections as optional
   enhancements and produce equivalent lifecycle outcomes.
8. Starting, advancing, completing, replaying, leaving, disconnecting, or cleaning one
   round cannot mutate another.
9. Existing per-round four-player capacity, Ready Golf barriers, scoring, ownership,
   camera, and travel behavior remain intact.
10. Vanilla-compatible action-bar/status output and the optional active HUD show accepted
    shots attempted, keep total strokes/penalties clear, reserve current-hole relative
    results for terminal state, and calculate active cumulative relative score from
    completed holes only.
11. Completed solo reconnect and scorecard delivery succeed without depending on global
   course selection.
12. Unit/build, command-path, Loom, Docker, installed-client, and two-client same-hole
    verification all pass.
